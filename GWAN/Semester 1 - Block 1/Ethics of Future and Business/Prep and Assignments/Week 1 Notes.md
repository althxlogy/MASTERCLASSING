- Theories categorized as  :
	- consequentialist: utility and outcomes (consequences)

	-  Limited view of the world and too abstract to apply to everything
- Normative ethical theories: they set a foundation prescription of what it means to be morally correct and how people **should behave**

- Ethical Absolutism: Claiming there are principles can be universally set and determined regardless of anything
- Ethical relativism: the opposite of this^ principles are relative and context dependent | no universal right or wrong
- Ethical Pluralism: middle ground that accepts values equally. Doesn't need one to favor each other

## Four Stage Ethical Decision Making Model
James Rest explains how people process moral issues into 4 steps: first they become aware that a moral issue needs to be addressed, then they judge the options and evaluate which one(s) would be morally right or wrong, then they establish their inner commitment and prioritize according to other interests, then they act based on their intentions
1. Awareness
2. Judgement
3. Intent - Evaluation
4. Action
